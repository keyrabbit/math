This is an area with significant documented user frustration:

3. **No erase option (in older versions):** The Australian reviewer encountered a situation where a mistaken answer during the assessment *couldn't be erased* and *couldn't be exited*, requiring a full app reinstall.

4. **Emotional impact of feedback:** When wrong, the app apparently delivers strong negative verbal feedback. One child reviewer wrote: *"it said horrible and I was just offended. They really need to stop saying mean stuff to kids when they get it wrong."* This specific wording ("horrible") suggests on-screen text labels for wrong answers, not just a buzz/red indicator.

### What "Japanese Methodology" Most Likely Refers To
The "Japanese methodology" framing is a common marketing claim in the EdTech drill-and-practice space. In this context, it almost certainly refers to **Kumon** (founded by Toru Kumon in Japan in 1954), which is the world's most widely-recognized Japanese math methodology, characterized by:
- Daily short worksheet-based drills
- Small incremental difficulty steps
- Self-paced mastery (must demonstrate fluency before advancing)
- Heavy repetition of arithmetic operations
- Minimal direct instruction; errors self-corrected through repetition

The Math Learner app's structure aligns strongly with Kumon principles: sequential "Books" by topic, mastery-before-advancement, drill repetition, minimal explanation when wrong ("not so much lessons, but really collections of drills" per Common Sense Media), and the star-rating system rewarding accuracy/speed.

**No claims of** Singapore Math (bar models, CPA approach), Common Core alignment, Montessori, or Finnish methodology were found in any official app materials. The ten-frame manipulative mode (Mode 2) does use a Singapore Math/Common Core-style manipulative, but the app does not claim Singapore Math branding.
